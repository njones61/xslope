# W-1 recorded sessions — what was asked, and what came back

Eight conversations with the Studio assistant, recorded against the live provider
(`anthropic` / `claude-opus-5`) by `tools/assistant_sessions.py`. Every turn was
audited afterwards: the workbook the session left behind was reloaded from disk
and re-solved independently, and each number the assistant reported was compared
against that run. This file is the producer's record — the prompts in order, the
files each session produced, and what the audit found.

Five of the eight — `build_from_image`, `sweep_adhoc`, `conceptual`, `diagnose`
and `report` — were recorded again after the brief revision of `28d2af34` and
the interface changes of `b837a592` and `c62a4928`: the brief now says to
measure before claiming a cause, to diagnose by varying one input at a time and
report the result as a ranked table naming what was not tested, to take the
reliability index from the engine rather than from the textbook definition, to
call `corpus_index` before answering a question that asks for a worked example,
and to write no LaTeX; the chat degrades LaTeX to readable text; a fence of prose
is never executed on a tool-calling provider; `generate_report` writes into the
chat's Files folder; and `run_lem` stores its result and names the surface it
solved on. Their prompts are unchanged. Each of those five sections ends with
**what changed** against the previous recording. The other three — `modify`,
`sweep_builtin`, `elastic_fem` — are the earlier recording and stand as they
were; none of them contains math, and the dock renders them as it did.

The base model is the LEM-8 reinforced slope
(`xslope_reinforced_slope.xlsx`): a 24 ft sand embankment at 1.25:1 with a 2 ft
cohesive face band, a 240 psf crest surcharge, and six geogrid layers at 800
lb/ft. Its published answer is **Spencer FS = 1.587** on a circle centered at
(−5.13, 46.98) with R = 47.26, and Bishop = 1.594. The model declares no LEM
method of its own, so `run_lem` called without one runs Spencer.

## 1. `w1_build_from_image` — build from the drawing

**Start:** a new, empty project (File → New).
**Attached:** `images/lem08_problem_sketch.png`.

> Build this model. Use the dimensions and properties on the drawing. Unit
> system: US customary (ft, psf, pcf). Add a starting circle and run Spencer with
> a search.

**Files:** `images/w1_build_from_image_1.png` (560 × 2912),
`w1_build_from_image_transcript.txt`, `w1_build_from_image_after.xlsx`.
**Cost:** 5 completions, 91,314 in (75,730 cached) / 15,102 out, 218.9 s.

**The geometry is the drawing, dimension for dimension.** It put the rigid base
at y = 0, the toe at (30, 10), the crest break at (60, 34) up a 1.25:1 face, and
the crest out to x = 130 — 130 ft overall, which is what the drawing states, and
30 ft of ground in front of the toe. That is LEM-8's own section translated 30 ft
right and 10 ft up. The cohesive face band was measured **2 ft horizontally**:
the shell polygon is (30,10)–(60,34)–(62,34)–(32,10), which is LEM-8's band
exactly. Both materials carry γ = 130 pcf and φ′ = 37°, with c′ = 300 psf on the
shell and 0 on the base. The 240 psf surcharge runs across the 70 ft crest from
x = 60 to 130. The six geosynthetic layers sit at y = 10, 14, 18, 22, 26 and 30,
each starting on the face, each 20 ft long, each at Tmax = 800 lb/ft with 4 ft of
pullout at both ends.

**It read the model checks and repaired what they found.** The first pass came
back with 13 errors — no failure surface, `Type = 'geotextile'` on all six
reinforcement lines, and a pullout delta out of range on all six. It set Type to
`geosynthetic`, cleared `adhesion` and `delta` to blank, called
`generate_starting_circles` for three circles from a center at (45, 58), and the
checks came back clean.

**Wrong: it invented values for two fields the template computes for itself.**
The reinforcement dicts it wrote carry `'dir': 0.0` and `'appl': ''`. In the
open template those two cells hold a VLOOKUP on Type, so neither is a value the
user supplies. Both invented values are read:

- `slice.py:1934` asks `rl.get("dir", "tangent") == "tangent"`, and `0.0` is not
  `"tangent"`, so all six lines were applied **along the bar axis** rather than
  tangent to the failure surface;
- a blank `appl` is not `'active'`, so the capacity was applied as **passive** —
  divided by the factor of safety.

Measured on the saved model, one field at a time:

| Dir | Appl | FS |
| :--- | :--- | ---: |
| tangent | active (the template's own) | **1.5881** |
| 0.0 | active | 1.6058 |
| tangent | blank | 1.4533 |
| 0.0 | blank (what the session ran) | **1.4710** |

So the pair costs 0.117, almost all of it the blank `appl`. And 1.5881 is the
answer LEM-8 publishes as 1.5867 — the two searches start from different circles
and settle 0.0014 apart. The model the assistant built is right; the two fields
it filled in are what moved the number.

**Wrong: the workbook it saved does not reopen.** `load_slope_data` refuses it —
`Reinforcement line 'Line 1' (reinforce sheet, Excel row 3) has an unrecognized
Dir='0.0'. Expected: tangent or axial.` The writer put the literal `0.0` into
the Dir cell (`fileio.py:3562`), replacing the lookup formula the template
carries there. Clearing that one cell on all six rows is the whole repair, and
the file then loads and solves at 1.5881.

**The input checks do not catch it.** `preflight`'s `reinforce.dir_vocabulary`
rule reads `str(r.get("dir") or "")`, and `0.0` is falsy, so a numeric Dir reads
as blank and passes. A string does not: setting `dir = 'sideways'` on the same
model raises six errors. The rule catches the wrong vocabulary and misses the
wrong type.

**Also worth a reader's eye.**

- **One of the five completions ran an empty snippet** and printed nothing.
- **It wrote E = 0 and ν = 0 on both materials.** Harmless for a limit
  equilibrium run, a singular stiffness matrix for any finite element run.
- **The geometry landed on the `polygon` sheet.** Profile lines is empty, and
  `max_depth` reads blank on reload — correct for polygon input, so the
  `max_depth = 0.0` it wrote is inert.

**What changed vs. the previous run.** The two readings the previous recording
got wrong are both right now: the face band is measured 2 ft horizontally rather
than 2 ft normal to the face, and the section is 130 ft as dimensioned rather
than the 150 ft the extent rule produced. The two engine-source dumps that opened
the previous turn are gone — 5 completions against 6, 91k input tokens against
106k — though one completion still ran an empty snippet. In their place is a new
failure of a different kind: two invented field values, worth 0.117 of factor of
safety, that also leave a workbook the reader cannot open.

## 3. `w1_sweep_builtin` — a parametric sweep

**Start:** the completed workbook.

> Sweep the geogrid Tmax from 500 to 3000 lb/ft in 6 steps with a search at each
> step and plot FS against Tmax.

**Files:** `images/w1_sweep_builtin_1.png`, `w1_sweep_builtin_transcript.txt`. No
workbook — the sweep restored the project, and the harness correctly wrote
nothing.
**Cost:** 2 completions, 26,868 in (25,044 cached) / 1,577 out, 104 s.

**Right, and cheap.** It used the preloaded `sensitivity(values, apply, ...)`
helper with `method='spencer', search=True`, wrote one CSV and one plot, and left
the model at Tmax = 800. Note that `list_params` carries **no reinforcement
refs** at all — Tmax is not a sweepable parameter reference — so the callback
helper is the correct tool here, not `design_sweep(param=...)`. Three of its six
steps were re-run independently and match to four figures:

| Tmax | assistant | independent re-run |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 500 | 1.425 | 1.4252 |
| 1000 | 1.641 | 1.6407 |
| 3000 | 1.681 | 1.6813 |

**The explanation for the plateau is not supported.** The assistant wrote that
past ~2000 lb/ft "the mobilizable force is capped by pullout/anchorage (the
`lp1`/`lp2` embedment terms)". Removing the pullout limit entirely (Lp1 = Lp2 =
0, fully anchored) at Tmax = 2000 raises FS only from 1.681 to **1.715** — 2%,
where the claim predicts the plateau should lift. What actually happens is that
the critical circle migrates: its center moves from (−7.74, 48.06) at Tmax = 500
to (5.69, 48.64) at Tmax = 3000, as the search finds a mechanism that engages
less reinforcement. The numbers are right and the shape of the curve is right;
the mechanism offered for it is a plausible story that a measurement contradicts.

## 5. `w1_elastic_fem` — stiffnesses, then a finite element run

**Start:** the completed workbook. Two turns.

> (a) Suggest values of Young's modulus and Poisson's ratio for these materials
> so I can run a finite element analysis, and explain your choice.
> (b) Enter them, build a quadratic mesh at 2 ft, and run the strength reduction
> analysis.

**Files:** `images/w1_elastic_fem_1.png`, `_2.png`,
`w1_elastic_fem_transcript.txt`, `w1_elastic_fem_after.xlsx`.
**Cost:** 5 completions, 71,104 in (62,610 cached) / 2,654 out, 175 s.

**Right, and the SSRM number is exact.** It called `suggest_elastic()` and
reported the classifier's answers verbatim (shell → Medium Clay, E = 668,300 psf,
ν = 0.40; base → Dense Sand, E = 2,861,300 psf, ν = 0.30), and it noticed that
both materials already carried a placeholder E = 1,000,000 / ν = 0.3. It then
argued — correctly — that a c = 300, φ = 37° material is a c–φ fill rather than a

classifier's clay value, saying so. Rebuilding the mesh from the saved file at
the same settings gives an identical mesh (4,364 nodes / 2,101 elements, tri6 at
2 ft) and an identical **SSRM FS = 1.47265625**.

Its statement that SSRM factor of safety is insensitive to E and ν, and that ν
should stay ≤ 0.45 in a drained run, is correct.

**Worth a reader's eye.** It flagged that it could not tell whether the six

`slope_data['mesh']` reports `element_type` as `None`, so its own read-back of

## 6. `w1_conceptual` — two questions that change nothing

**Start:** the completed workbook. Two turns. No workbook written (correct).

> (a) How does a reliability analysis work in XSLOPE?
> (b) How do I decide standard deviations for a reliability analysis if I only
> have a few tests?

**Files:** `images/w1_conceptual_1.png`, `_2.png`,
`w1_conceptual_transcript.txt`.
**Cost:** 3 completions, 52,009 in (45,438 cached) / 4,075 out, 60.6 s.

**It consults the corpus, and it takes the index from the engine.** The first
snippet is `corpus_index('reliability')` beside a read of the `sigma_*` fields on
both materials. Ten rows came back and it cited four of them by name — LEM sample
15, VP29 (Duncan's LASH terminal, Taylor against Monte Carlo), VP33 (the
five-material dike) and FEM sample 4 — all real pages, all reachable at the URLs
it printed. The second turn cites three of the same rows again.

On the index it now writes: "XSLOPE reports the **lognormal** reliability index,
`beta_ln` … Do not compute it as `(E[FS] − 1)/σ_FS` — that is the normal-variate
form and gives a different number than the run returns." That is what the engine
does.

**Right on the engines.** Its three-engine table matches the implementation,
including the surface treatment: Taylor re-searches on every one of its 1 + 2N
solves, Monte Carlo and the response surface hold one surface fixed
(`reliability_mc`'s own docstring: "The failure surface is never randomized").
Every helper it names is real and correctly spelled, with the right keyword
arguments — `reliability_taylor(method=…)`, `reliability_mc(method=…,
n_samples=…, rng_seed=…)`, `reliability_rs(method=…)`, and the
`reliability(engine=…)` front door.

**Wrong: what an all-zero-σ model does.** It writes that "a reliability run right
now would return σ_FS = 0 and no meaningful β". Run on this model,
`reliability_taylor` returns `(False, 'Reliability analysis requires standard
deviations for at least one material property. None were provided: s(g), s(c),
s(f) and s(c/p) are blank or zero on every material …')`. It refuses; it does not
return anything. This is the one claim about the tool left in the answer that the
tool was not asked.

**Wrong: one conversion it did by hand.** "For φ′ = 37° and COV(tan φ′) = 10%,
tan 37° = 0.754, σ_tanφ = 0.075, and the corresponding spread in degrees is about
37° ± 2.4°, so `sigma_phi ≈ 2.4`." The first two numbers are right and the third
is not: 0.0754 on tan 37° is 2.75° in degrees, and the bracket runs 34.15° to
39.66°.

**Sound practice content, and it says what it did not run.** Duncan's three-sigma
rule with the /4 caution, published COV bands, the sample standard deviation as a
lower bound with few tests, spatial averaging along the surface, and the advice
to run the analysis at a low and a high σ rather than argue about the estimate —
followed by "I have not run that here", which is the sentence the brief asks for.

**What changed vs. the previous run.** The two failures the previous recording
had are both fixed, and both were brief changes: `corpus_index` is called
unprompted and four worked examples are cited by name where the previous run
cited none, and the reliability index is the engine's `beta_ln` where the
previous run defined the normal-variate form and worked a micro-example to a
number the tool does not report. The zero-σ prediction survives in a softer form.
One new slip, in the only arithmetic it did by hand.

## 7. `w1_diagnose` — a model broken on purpose

**Start:** `w1_diagnose_start.xlsx` — the completed LEM-8 model with three
transcription errors written into it by the producer:

1. material 2 (`base`) φ = **3°** instead of 37 (a dropped digit);
2. the crest surcharge = **2400 psf** instead of 240 (a decimal slip);
3. `max_depth` = **−100** instead of −10 (a dropped digit).

Spencer with a search returns 0.071 on it.

> This model gives a factor of safety below 1. Can you find what is wrong?

**Files:** `images/w1_diagnose_1.png` (560 × 6961),
`w1_diagnose_transcript.txt`. No workbook — it changed nothing and said so, and
the harness confirms it by writing none.
**Cost:** 9 completions, 191,949 in (136,314 cached) / 11,209 out, 784.1 s.

A first attempt at the same prompt ran 12 completions and reached the harness's
900 s per-turn cap in the middle of its measurements, before it wrote an answer;
that recording was discarded and the session re-run on an otherwise idle machine.

**All three faults are found, and none is invented.** It read the model once, ran
the baseline search, and then varied one input at a time across four more
snippets — φ at 10, 20, 30 and 37; the surcharge at 240 and removed; `max_depth`
at 0; the reinforcement removed; and two versions of a regrouped material zoning.
The answer is a ranked table of ten rows, followed by a numbered diagnosis and a
section headed **What I did not test**.

Every number in the table reproduces independently:

| Change | assistant | independent re-run |
| :--- | ---: | ---: |
| baseline | 0.071 | 0.0709, circle (−3.09, 49.09), R 45.93 |
| `base` φ 3 → 30 | 0.914 | 0.9138 |
| `base` φ 3 → 37 | 1.181 | 1.1805 |
| surcharge 2400 → 240 | 0.096 | 0.0964 |
| surcharge removed | 0.103 | 0.1026 |
| `max_depth` −100 → 0 | 0.070 | 0.0709 at −10, the value that was meant |
| φ = 30 + 240 psf | 1.222 | 1.2218 |

Its infinite-slope check is right too: the face is 30H:24V, β = 38.66°, and
tan 3° / tan 38.7° = 0.066 against the 0.071 it measured. The equation arrives as
plain text in a blockquote, not as LaTeX.

**Fault 1 is named as the fault**, ranked first, on the grounds that it is the
only input that moves the factor of safety by more than a hundredth. It reads it
as a dropped digit and recommends φ = 30 — the file was broken from 37, and it
measured 37 → 1.181 and listed it in the table, so the reader has both.

**Fault 2 is named as the same kind of typo**, ranked second, with its worth
measured both ways: +0.025 on its own, and 0.914 → 1.222 once φ is restored.

**Fault 3 is de-ranked by measurement**, not by argument: `max_depth` → 0 returns
0.070 against the baseline 0.071. It adds that a base 100 ft below a 24 ft
section is invented depth and should be 0 whatever the factor of safety does.

**The face band is no longer a bug it invented.** The regrouping the previous
recording named as the cause is here a hypothesis it measured and hedged: 0.129
alone, 1.353 with φ = 30, and "I could not tell from the model which was
intended", listed third and called secondary.

**Wrong: the claim that the reinforcement contributes nothing.** It writes "FS is
identical to three decimals with and without them, both at the baseline (0.071)
and at the repaired case (1.222 either way). I measured that." The measurement
was a no-op: it set `reinforcement_lines = []`, and the slicer falls back to the
derived `reinforce_lines` when the primary list is empty (`slice.py:1211`), so
all six lines were still in the model. Clearing both lists takes the broken model
from **0.0709 to 0.0312**, and the repaired case from **1.2218 to 0.9358**. The
same removal on the LEM-8 model gives 1.1674, which is LEM-8's published
no-reinforcement answer.

**One number in the table carries an admissibility problem it did not pass on.**
The regrouped-geometry run at 0.129 reported 147 of 528 trial surfaces unsolved
with **68 of them ranking below the reported minimum**, plus interslice tension
and a line of thrust outside the slice on 95% of boundaries. It quoted 0.129 in
the table without the qualification, having quoted the baseline's admissibility
notes in full.

**Two smaller things.** It hit the polygon-edit warning on its first geometry
test, read it, and redid the test through `profile_lines` — the same
self-correction session 2 shows. And the section headed "Two things you should
know" lists three.

**What changed vs. the previous run.** This is the session the brief was revised
for, and it is a different answer. The previous recording read the model twice,
ran one search, reasoned from the printed values, accepted the planted φ = 3° as
the design, and named the 2 ft cohesive face band as the cause. This one varies
one input at a time, reports what restoring each was worth in numbers, names all
three planted faults, demotes the face-band hypothesis to a measured and hedged
third place, and states what it could not test. It costs four times as much
(191,949 input tokens against 47,432) and takes six times as long.

Every number the assistant reported was reproducible. Across the eight sessions
that is more than twenty separate factors of safety, the SSRM factor of safety to
eight digits, and every critical-circle center, each one matching an independent
re-run. **Not one arithmetic or solver-level error.**

What is left divides into two kinds. The first is a claim about the tool that the
tool was not asked — what a zero-σ reliability run returns (session 6), a
mechanism offered for a curve (session 3). The second is newer and harder to
catch: **a measurement that measured nothing**. The diagnosis removed the
reinforcement by clearing one of the two lists the slicer reads, saw no change,
and reported it as measured (session 7); the build session filled two fields the
template computes for itself, which cost 0.117 of factor of safety and left a
workbook that will not reopen (session 1). Neither reads as a guess. Both are
caught the same way — by reloading the file and running it.

That is the lesson the page has to teach: the assistant computes correctly, and
what it computed is not always what it said it measured. Check the model, check
the method, and open the file.
